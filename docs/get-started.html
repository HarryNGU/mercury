Get started

Set up Mercury in about two minutes

Install it, say hello, and Mercury walks you through the rest. Everything after installing is optional.

1. Install

  1. Download Mercury

    From the download button. Your browser saves a file called Mercury-1.1.0.dmg.

  2. Drag Mercury into Applications

    Open the downloaded file and drag the Mercury icon onto the Applications folder.

  3. Open it

    Open Mercury from Applications.

    Mercury is signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens like any other app.

    The first time, macOS asks you to confirm: open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway next to Mercury. You only do this once.

2. The welcome tour

The first time you open Mercury, it introduces itself in a short chat. It asks what to call you (that's optional, and stays on your Mac), lets you pick what you'd like help with, then asks for each permission one at a time, explaining why before macOS shows its own prompt. You can skip any of them. Mercury only asks again when you use that feature.

PermissionWhy Mercury asksNeeded for
Screen RecordingTo record your screen, to take a screenshot when you ask Mercury about it, and to hear your Mac's sound when you turn on Listen.Record, Ask, Listen
CameraFor the round camera bubble in recordings. Never used otherwise.Record (optional)
MicrophoneFor your voice in recordings, dictation and when you talk to Mercury.Record, Dictate, Talk
Speech RecognitionTo turn speech into text on your Mac.Dictate, Ask Mercury, captions
AccessibilityTo notice the ⌥⌥ double tap in other apps and type text where your cursor is.Dictate, Ask Mercury messages
AutomationAsked only if you have Ask Mercury use an app such as Mail or Messages for you.Ask Mercury actions

After allowing Screen Recording, macOS may ask you to quit and reopen Mercury. Your choices are saved. You can review or remove any permission later in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

3. Connect your AI (optional)

Ask Mercury needs an AI to answer your questions. The tour asks which one you'd like to use, or you can skip it:

  • Claude Code on your Mac (recommended). If you already use Claude Code, Ask Mercury uses it with your own Claude plan. It brings your memory notes and your connected apps (like Gmail and Calendar). Sign in once by opening Terminal, running claude and typing /login.
  • An Anthropic API key. Create one at console.anthropic.com and paste it into Mercury. It's stored in your Mac's Keychain and billed to your Anthropic account.
  • Not now. Recording and dictation work without any AI.

Other assistants such as ChatGPT or Gemini can't be connected yet.

Before Ask Mercury can see or hear anything, it shows you its ground rules and asks you to agree. You can read them on the safety page.

4. Sharper voice and captions (optional)

Whisper is a free speech model that runs on your Mac and is much more accurate than the one built into macOS. To use it, install Homebrew, run brew install whisper-cpp in Terminal, then download the model from Mercury's welcome tour or reel editor (574 MB, once). Nothing is uploaded.

5. Shortcuts

Click any shortcut inside Mercury to change it.

Uninstall

Mercury doesn't keep anything outside your Mac, so this removes it completely:

  1. Quit Mercury and drag it from Applications to the Bin.
  2. Optional: delete its data folder, ~/Library/Application Support/Bubble, and its log, ~/Library/Logs/Bubble.log. Your recordings in ~/Desktop/Bubble Recordings are yours to keep or delete.
  3. Optional: if you saved an API key, open the Keychain Access app, search for "Bubble AI polish" and delete it.
  4. Optional: remove Mercury from System Settings → Privacy & Security lists (Screen Recording, Microphone and so on).
